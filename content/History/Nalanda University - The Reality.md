When we talk about History, there are some conspiracy theorists who have their own type of history to be presented, slighly or wholly different than the one which is academically & globally accepted.

But what if I say that there's one conspiracy theory which actually got a platform & won over the real history. This might be India's biggest historical gaslighting which made its way to the textbooks & got accepted as history when it was just a misunderstanding of what the primary source mentioned.

Nalanda Mahavira, one of the biggest educational institutes in India between the 5th and 12th centuries and probably the world's first residential university, was established in the 5th century by Emperor Kumaragupta I in modern-day Bihar, India. It served as a premier center for Buddhist & Vedic learning for over 800 years, hosting 10,000 students and 2,000 teachers before its destruction.

But how did it get Destroyed? And who destroyed Nalanda? 

If you do a google search, you'll find tons of websites and articles saying that it was the Turkic invader Bakhtiyar Khilji who set fire to Nalanda in 1197-98 AD and the fire lasted about 12-15 days while some articles say that it continued burning for 3-4 months.

But wait a minute, this was Nalanda, not a forest in Africa that could burn for months.

![[Nalanda_Mahavira.jpg]]
> [!info] Who was Bakhtiyar Khilji
> **Muhammad Bakhtiyar Khilji** was a **Turkic military commander** (sipahsalar) of the Khalaj tribe who served under the Ghurid expansion in northern India during the late twelfth century, associated with the rule of **Muhammad Ghori**.

But what if I say that Khilji was not the one who destroyed Nalanda. In fact, the claim that Nalanda kept burning for 3 months is not just a blatantly false claim but rather it's the biggest propaganda of Indian History.

Yes, this can be said that Khilji was an evil person, and so was Ghori, but if we just go through the truth, you'll find it very different from the current cinematic ending of Nalanda.

Here's a well documented refutation of the very claim that Bakhtiyar destroyed Nalanda.

### Excavation Report doesn't talk about Fire
The excavation report of Nalanda Mahavira does not talk about any massive fire.

### There's no Primary source/evidence
There's not a single Historical source which says that Bakhtiyar Khilji burnt Nalanda.
There's only one eyewitness account of Khilji's time we have, & that is a Persian book - 'Tabakat-e-Nasiri' written in the 13th Century by Minhaj-us-Siraj Zuzjani.

![[SmartSelect_20260922_205643_WPS Office.jpg|center|386]]

> Translation:- Trustworthy persons have related on this wise, that he advanced to the gateway of the fortress of Bihar with two hundred horsemen in defensive armour, and suddenly attacked the place. There were two brothers of Farghanah, men of learning, one Nizam-ud-Din, the other Samsam-ud-Din \[by name], in the service of Mubammadi-Bakht-yar: and the author of this book met with Samsam-ud-Din at Lakhanawati in the year 641 H., and this account is from him. These two wise brothers were soldiers among that band of holy warriors when they reached the gateway of the fortress and began the attack, at which time Muhammad-i-Bakht-yar. by the force of his intrepidity, threw himself into the postern of the gateway of the place, and they captured the fortress, and acquired great booty. The greater number of the inhabitants of that place were Brahmans, and the whole of those Brahmans had their heads shaven; and they were all slain. There were a great number of books’ there; and, when all these books came under the observation of the Musalmans, they summoned a number of Hindus that they might give them information respecting the import of those books; but the whole of the Hindus had been killed. On becoming aquainted \[with the contents of those books], it was found that the whole of that fortress and city was a college, add in the Hindui tongue, they call a college Bihar.

In this book, Minhaj wrote that, in 641 Hijri (1243-44 CE), two army companions of Bakhtiyar Khilji (Nizam & Samsam) informed him that Khilji attacked (not burnt) Bihar Fort (Kila-e-Bihar).
 
In fact, in the same book, Minhaj mentions all the places that Md. Ghori and his army conquered; nowhere does he mention Nalanda Mahavira, instead, he mentions Adanda Vihar

![[SmartSelect_20260922_233222_YouTube.jpg|center|296]] ![[SmartSelect_20260922_233421_WPS Office.jpg|center|301]]

What's Adanda Vihar? Historian Jadunath Sarkar in his book 'History of Bengal' mentions that Adanda Vihar is nothing but **Odantapuri Monestry** & that's what Bakhtiyar captured.

![[SmartSelect_20260922_234601_Chrome.jpg|center|633x344]]

And further in Minhaj's book he writes that after conquering Odantapuri Monestry, Khilji came to Delhi. 
There's zero mention of Bakhtiyar attacking any place known as Nalanda or setting fire to any place he conquered which burnt for a single minute.
In fact, after meeting Aibak in Delhi, Khilji went to Lakhanawati, Bengal. Minhaj writes that the King of Lakhanawati-Raja Lakhsman Sen fled bare-footed by the back part of his palace when Khilji attacked his palace. And this Lakhanawati is mentioned right after Adanda Bihar in Minhaj's book.

Clearly, the attack was done on Odantapuri Monestry (in around 1197-98 CE) & not Nalanda, and there's no mention either that he set fire on Odantapuri Monestry.

### Buddhist Monk
The second contradiction comes from a Tibbetan-Buddhist monk Dharmaswamin. According to him, Nalanda was still functional in the 13th Century & he studied there.

Dharmaswamin came to India in 1234 CE to study Buddhist texts and spent time in Nalanda as well. He says Nalanda was still standing in 1235–36 CE, and interestingly, his records make no mention of any destruction or great fire that might have scorched it. Yes, he definitely points out that the *funding for the maintenance of Nalanda's Patronage University was reduced* a lot. That's why many structures were in need of repair.

Nalanda's head teacher Rahul Shribhadra was being supported by a local king.

In fact, Dharmaswamin writes that teaching had been functional in Nalanda for the past 40 years. In his book, Dharmasvamin also notes that raids by the 'Turushkas' took place at Nalanda after his time, specifically after 1234 CE. Who were these Turushkas? Many people identify them as Turkish invaders. However, in his book on Buddhist monasteries, historian Sukumar Dutt writes that Turushka invasions of Nalanda were being reported from a time when no Turks were even present in India. 

![[Screenshot_20260923_123333_Samsung capture.jpg]]

For instance, Dutt cites an account from the seventh century involving King Chandrakirti, who was the head of Nalanda at the time, in which he repelled an attack on Nalanda by the 'Turushkas'. Now, there were no Turks in India in the 7th century; the term 'Turushka' has historically been used to refer to 'invaders' in general. However, if Dharmasvamin was indeed using this term specifically for Turks, then interestingly, he notes that these 'Turushkas' came to raid Nalanda *from the Odantapuri monastery* after 1234. This clears the very fact again that Odantapuri was military base of the Turks, which was conquered by Bakhtiyar Khilji & not Nalanda.

### Where did Fire Theory come from? & who set fire?
For this we have 2 Tibbetan-Buddhist sources.
First is from Taranath's accounts of the 17th Century, in which he blames Brahmins for burning Nalanda & not Bakhtiyar Khilji

![[Taranatha’s History of Buddhism in India_0170.jpg|center|633x532]]

Kakutasiddha (minister of the king) built a temple in Nalendra (Nalanda). He arranged a great ceremonial feast. In this feast, two tirthikas (brahmins) came but 2 shramanas (Buddhist monks) threw water on them.

One tirthika decided to take revenge as this was a big insult to him. He started taking sun's siddhi (power) but failed because *siddhi ain't real*.
Both tirthikas did a Yajna & threw its ashes on Nalanda due to which 84 Buddhist Centers got burnt & many books of Ratnodadhi & Ratnasagara Library also got burnt.
Then these 2 brahmins fled to Hasama (Assam).

In fact this same account is mentioned in an 18th Century Tibbetan-Buddhist source written by Sumpa Khan-Po. He writes exact same story & blames brahmins for burning Nalanda.
![[shot_20260314_001117.jpg]]
There's not a single source up till the 18th Century which says that Khilji burnt Nalanda.

#### Excavation Report of Nalanda
Excavation Report of Nalanda by D.R. Patil says that there is some proof of partial burning and ash deposits are present in the upper layer.
An inscription found from 1003 AD at site 12 details that a massive fire destroyed Nalanda and a person named Baladitya restored some of its parts during the reign of Mahipala Deva.

![[shot_20260314_003107.jpg]]

So Nalanda was already burnt at least once, 200 years before the arrival of Turks in India. D.R. Patil says that Nalanda faced several accidental fires because cooking used to be done in closed rooms of Nalanda most often & monks used uncovered lamps due to which accidental fires were common.

### The Real Decline of Nalanda
It is quite clear that Bakhtiyar Khilj was not the one who burnt Nalanda as Nalanda was functional uptill 1234 CE according to Dharmaswamin.

According to scholars, the decline of Nalanda was slow & gradual, and its effects were visible from the 9th-10th Century.

History is quite the opposite of Cinema; we see less 'sudden ends' and more slow and gradual ends.

According to Patil, Nalanda's slow end was almost complete in the 11th Century, almost a hundred years before Khilji's arrival.

The major reason for the decline of Nalanda was the making of new Universities (Vihāra) such as Odantapuri Monestry, Vikramshila & Sompuri by Pala Dynasty ruler Dharampal & his successor Devpāla II.

So the patronage that had previously been given to Nalanda was now given to these new universities.

According to Sukumar Dutt, because of these new Universities, many scholars left Nalanda to join these new Universities. Also, there was a lot of competition among different factions of Buddhism, especially *Vajrayana* which involves tantrik practices, & Palas used to patronise such practices, and they also tried to make Nalanda a learning hub of such tantrik practices

![[SmartSelect_20260925_194408_YouTube.jpg]]

And thus liberal & free learning was reduced in Nalanda.

RS Sharma says monks were living feudally in Nalanda and other viharas, keeping wealth mostly for themselves rather than for the university. This had a negative impact on Nalanda. Because of this greed, social inequality also increased, and the local population stopped patronizing Nalanda, which further contributed to its decline.

This is also confirmed by Dharmaswamin, as when he came to Nalanda, it had mostly declined and many places needed repairs which were not happening.

This is how Nalanda ultimately declined and there was no hand of Khilji in this, instead he attacked Odantapuri Monestry and made it his military base; today we call it *Bihar Sharif*.

This is believed by many scholars, such as D.N. Jha, Sukumar Dutt, RS Sharma, Dr Patil, Jadunath Sarkar, R.C. Majumdar.

The only mentions of fire in Nalanda come from a hundred years before the arrival of the Turks in India, or from Tibetan-Buddhist sources, which blame the Brahmins for it. But none of such things ended Nalanda, Nalanda's end happened because of lack of funds, corruption, & most of all because of an authoritarian government.
