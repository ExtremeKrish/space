When we talk about History, there are some conspiracy theorists which have their own type of history to be presented, slighly or wholly different than the one which is academically & globally accepted.

But what if I say that there's one conspiracy theory which actually got a platform & won over the real history. This might be India's biggest historical gaslighting which made its way to the textbooks & got accepted as history when it was just misunderstanding of what the primary source mentioned.

Nalanda Mahavira was one of the biggest educational institute of India in 5th-12th century. It was established in the 5th Century by Emperor Kumaragupta I, probably the world's first residential university, located in modern-day Bihar, India. It served as a premier center for Buddhist & Vedic learning for over 800 years, hosting 10,000 students and 2,000 teachers before its destruction.

But how it got Destroyed? And who destyroyed Nalanda? 

If you do a google search, you'll find tons of websites and articles saying that it was the Turkic invador Bakhtiyar Khilji who set fire on Nalanda in 1193/1198CE and the fire lasted about 12-15 days, some articles say that it continued burning for 3-4 months.
But wait a min, that was Nalanda not Forests of Africa which would kept burning for this huge timespan.

![[Nalanda_Mahavira.jpg|474]]

> [!info] Who was Bakhtiyar Khilji
> **Muhammad Bakhtiyar Khilji** was a **Turkic military commander** (sipahsalar) of the Khalaj tribe who served under the Ghurid expansion in northern India during the late twelfth century, associated with the rule of **Muhammad Ghori**.

But what if I say that Khilji was not the one who destroyed Nalanda, infact the claim that nalanda kept burning for 3 months is not just a blatantly false claim but rather it's the biggest propaganda of Indian History.

Yes this can be said that Khilji was an evil person, Ghoi was an evil person, but if we just go through the truth you'll find it very different from the current cinematic ending of Nalanda.

Here's a well documented refutation of the very claim that Bakhtiyar destroyed Nalanda.

### Excavation Report dont talks about Fire
The excavation report of Nalanda Mahavira does not talk about any massive fire.

### There's no Primary source/evidence
There's not a single Historical source which says that Bakhtiyar Khilji burnt Nalanda.
There's only one eyewitness account of Khilji's time we have, & that is a persian book - 'Tabakat-e-Nasiri' written in 13th Century by Minhaj-us-Siraj Zuzjani.

![[SmartSelect_20260922_205643_WPS Office.jpg|386]]
> Translation:- Trustworthy persons have related on this wise, that he advanced to the gateway of the fortress of Bihar with two hundred horsemen in defensive armour, and suddenly attacked the place. There were two brothers of Farghanah, men of learning, one Nizam-ud-Din, the other Samsam-ud-Din \[by name], in the service of Mubammadi-Bakht-yar: and the author of this book met with Samsam-ud-Din at Lakhanawati in the year 641 H., and this account is from him. These two wise brothers were soldiers among that band of holy warriors when they reached the gateway of the fortress and began the attack, at which time Muhammad-i-Bakht-yar. by the force of his intrepidity, threw himself into the postern of the gateway of the place, and they captured the fortress, and acquired great booty. In this book, Minhaj wrotes that, in 641 Hijri (1243-44 CE), two army companions of Bakhtiyar Khilji (Nizam & Samsam) informed him that Khilji attacked (not burnt) Bihar Fort (Kila-e-Bihar).
 
Infact, in the same book, Minaj mentions all the places which Md. Gauri & his Sipah-e-saraj conquered, they also dont mention any *Nalanda Mahavira*, instead, it mentions **Adanda Vihar**.![[SmartSelect_20260922_233222_YouTube.jpg|296]] ![[SmartSelect_20260922_233421_WPS Office.jpg|301]]

What's Adanda Vihar? Historian Jadunath Sarkar in his book 'History of Bengal' mentions that Adanda Vihar is nothing but **Odantapuri Monestry** & that's what Bakhtiyar captured.

![[SmartSelect_20260922_234601_Chrome.jpg]]

And further in Minhaj's book he writes that after conquering Odantapuri Monestry, Khilji came to Delhi. 
There's zero mention of Bakhtiyar attacking any place known as Nalanda or setting fire to any place he conquered which burnt for a single minute.
Infact, after meeting Aibak in Delhi, Khilji went to Lakhanawati, Bengal. Minhaj writes that King of Lakhanawati-Raja Lakhsman Sen fled bare-footed by the back part of his palace when Khilji attacked his palace. And this Lakhanawati is mensioned right after Adanda Bihar in Minhaj's book.

Clearly, the attack was done on Odantapuri Monestry (in around 1197-98 CE) & not Nalanda, and there's no mention either that he sat fire on Odantapuri Monestry.
### Buddhist Monk
The second contradiction comes from a Tibbetan-Buddhist monk Dharmaswamin, according to him, Nalanda was still functional in 13th Century & he studied from there.

Dharmaswamin came in India in 1234 CE to study Buddhist texts, & spend time in Nalanda too, he says that nalanda was still standing in 1235-6 CE, intrerestingly in his records there's no mention of Destruction or Great-Fire that might have scorched Nalanda. Yes, he definitely points out that the funding for the maintenance of Nalanda's Patronage University was reduced a lot. That's why many structures were in need of repair.

Nalanda's head teacher Rahul Shribhadra was being supported by a local king.

Infact, Dharmaswamin writes that teaching was functional in Nalanda from past 40 years. In his book, Dharmasvamin also notes that raids by the 'Turushkas' took place at Nalanda after his time, specifically after 1234 CE. Who were these Turushkas? Many people identify them as Turkish invaders. However, in his book on Buddhist monasteries, historian Sukumar Dutt writes that Turushka invasions of Nalanda were being reported from a time when no Turks were even present in India. 

![[Screenshot_20260923_123333_Samsung capture.jpg]]
For instance, Dutt cites an account from the seventh century involving King Chandrakirti, who was the head of Nalanda at the time, where he repelled an attack on Nalanda by the 'Turushkas'. Now, there were no Turks in India in the 7th century; the term 'Turushka' has historically been used to refer to 'invaders' in general. However, if Dharmasvamin was indeed using this term specifically for Turks, then interestingly, he notes that these 'Turushkas' came to raid Nalanda *from the Odantapuri monastery* after 1234. This clears the very fact again that Odantapuri was military base of turks, which was conquered by Bakhtiyar Khilji & not Nalanda.

### Where did Fire Theory come from? & who sat fire?
For this we have 2 Tibbetan-Buddhist sources,
First is from Taranath's accounts of 17th Century, where he blames Brahmins for burning Nalanda & not Bakhtiyar Khilji 
![[Taranatha’s History of Buddhism in India_0170.jpg]]

Kakutasiddha (minister of king) built a temple in Nalendra (Nalanda), he arranged a great ceremonial feast, in this feast two tirthika (brahmins) came but 2 shramanas (Buddhist monk) threw water on them.

One tirthika decides to take revenge as this was a big insult to him, he started taking sun's siddhi (power) but failed because *siddhi ain't real*.
Both tirthikas did a Yajna & threw its ashes on Nalanda due to which 84 Buddhist Centers got burnt & many books of Ratnodadhi & Ratnasagara Library also got burnt.
Then these 2 brahmins flew Hasama (Assam).

Infact this same account is mentioned in 18th Century Tibbetan-Buddhist source written by Sumpa Khan-Po, he writes exact same story & blames brahmins for burning Nalanda.

There's not a single source uptill 18th Century which says that Khilji burnt Nalanda.
#### Excavation Report of Nalanda
Excavation Report of Nalanda by D.R. Patil says that there are some proofs of partial burning, ash deposits are present in upper layer.
An inscription found from 1003 AD in site 12 details that a massive fire destroyed Nalanda and a person named Baladitya restored some of its parts in th erule of Mahipala Deva.

So Nalandawas already burnt atleast once, 200 years before the arrival of Turks in India. D.R. Patil says that Nalanda faced several accidental fires because cooking used to done in closed rooms of Nalanda most often & monks used uncovered lamps due to which accidental fires were common.
