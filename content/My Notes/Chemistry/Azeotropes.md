# 🧪 **AZEOTROPES – FULL NOTES**

## 🔹 Definition

- **Azeotropes** are **binary liquid mixtures** that:
    
    - have **same composition in liquid and vapour phase**
        
    - boil at a **constant temperature**
        
- Hence, they behave like a **pure substance during boiling**
    

---

## 🔹 Key Property

- Vapour composition = Liquid composition
    
- Therefore, **cannot be separated by fractional distillation**
    

---

## 🔹 Important Concept (MOST IMPORTANT ⚠️)

👉 Azeotrope exists at **ONLY ONE specific composition**

- At that composition → vapour = liquid ✅
    
- If composition changes → vapour ≠ liquid ❌ (no longer azeotrope)
    

---

### ✔ Example:

- Ethanol + Water  
    → azeotrope at **~95% ethanol**
    
- Nitric acid + Water  
    → azeotrope at **68% HNO₃ + 32% H₂O**
    

---

## 🔹 Relation with Vapour Pressure & Boiling Point

- **High vapour pressure → Low boiling point**
    
- **Low vapour pressure → High boiling point**
    

---

## 🔹 Types of Azeotropes

---

## 🔥 1. Minimum Boiling Azeotrope

### ➤ Cause:

- Shows **positive deviation from Raoult’s law**
    
- A–B interactions are **weaker** than A–A and B–B
    

### ➤ Effect:

- Molecules escape easily  
    → Vapour pressure = **high**  
    → Boiling point = **low**
    

### ➤ Result:

- Mixture boils at **lower temperature than both components**
    

### ➤ Example:

- Ethanol + Water  
    → ~95% ethanol azeotrope
    

---

## ❄️ 2. Maximum Boiling Azeotrope

### ➤ Cause:

- Shows **negative deviation from Raoult’s law**
    
- A–B interactions are **stronger**
    

### ➤ Effect:

- Molecules held tightly  
    → Vapour pressure = **low**  
    → Boiling point = **high**
    

### ➤ Result:

- Mixture boils at **higher temperature than both components**
    

### ➤ Example:

- Nitric acid + Water  
    → 68% HNO₃ azeotrope (b.p. 393.5 K)
    

---

## 🔹 Why Separation Fails

- In normal mixtures:
    
    - Vapour ≠ liquid → separation possible
        
- In azeotropes:
    
    - Vapour = liquid → **no change during distillation**
        
    - So separation **stops at azeotropic composition**
        

---

## 🔹 Summary Table

|Type|Deviation|A–B Force|Vapour Pressure|Boiling Point|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Minimum boiling|Positive|Weak|High|Low|
|Maximum boiling|Negative|Strong|Low|High|

---

## 🧠 Final Memory Lines

- Azeotrope = **same liquid & vapour composition**
    
- Exists at **one fixed ratio only**
    
- Positive deviation → **minimum boiling**
    
- Negative deviation → **maximum boiling**
