## Additional Compound
1. **Double Salts**
	Additional Compounds which ionises & lose their identity in aq. Solution
E.g. $$
\ce{KCl(aq) + MgCl2(aq) ->[\Delta][Evaporation] KCl.MgCl2.6H2O} 
$$
2. **Coordination Compounds**
	Additional Compounds which don't lose their identity in aq. Solution.
	E.g. $$
	\ce{4KCN(aq) + Fe(CN)2(aq) ->[\Delta][Evaporation] K4[Fe(CN)6]} 
	$$
	The product we got here is called Complex salt Or Coordination Compound. Now if we put this in water, *chapaak*, we get $\ce{4K+ + [Fe(CN)6}]^{4-}$  so:
	- It don't completely ionise
	- It doesn't looses it's identity
	- All the ions dont give their positive test (like can u test Fe? no)

## Terminologies of CC
### a) Coordination Entity/Sphere
A coordination entity constitutes a *central metal atom or ion* bonded to a fixed number of ions or molecules (ligands).
### b) Central Atom/Ion
In a coordination entity, the atom/ion to which a fixed number of ions/groups are bound in a definite geometrical arrangement around it, is called the central atom or ion.

Those **species which donate electrons to** vacant d-orbitals or **central metal atom/ion through coordinate bond are known as ligands.**

**Denticity**
The number of co-ordinate bonds that a ligand can form with metal. 
Or Denticity refers to ==the number of donor atoms in a single ligand that bind directly to a central metal ion in a coordination complex==.

**Chelate**
When a di- or polydentate ligand uses its two or more donor atoms simultaneously to bind a single metal ion, it is said to be a chelate ligand.

#### Types of Ligands on the basis of Denticity
1. **Unidendate/Monodentate Ligands**
	When **a** ligand is bound to a metal ion *through a single donor atom*, as with $\ce{Cl-}$, $\ce{H2O}$ or $\ce{NH3}$, the ligand is said to be unidentate.
	E.g.: hexaamminecobalt(III) ion $\ce{[CO(NH3)6]^3+}$
	![[Pasted image 20260430150711.png|297x199]]
2. **Didentate or Bidentate Ligands**
	When a ligand can bind *through two donor atoms* as in $\ce{H2NCH2CH2NH2}$ (ethane-1,2-diamine/ethylenediamine) or $\ce{C2O4^2-}$ (oxalate), the ligand is said to be didentate.
	![[Pasted image 20260430151420.png|281x212]]
3. **Polydentate Ligands**
	When several donor atoms are present in a single ligand as in $\ce{N(CH2CH2NH2)3}$ (Tris(2-aminoethyl)amine), the ligand is said to be polydentate. 
	*Ethylenediaminetetraacetate ion (EDTA4–) is an important hexadentate ligand.* (Q)
	![[Pasted image 20260430152755.jpg|329x209]]
4. **Ambidentate Ligands**
	Ligand which has two different donor atoms and either of the two ligetes in the complex is called ambidentate ligand. 
	Examples of such ligands are the $\ce{NO2^-}$ and $\ce{SCN-}$ ions.

### f) Oxidation Number of Central Atom
The oxidation number of the central atom in a complex is defined as the charge it would carry if all the ligands are removed along with the electron pairs that are shared with the central atom.

### g) Homolyptic/Heterolyptic/Homonuclear Complexes
1. Homolyptic complexes → Complexes in which a metal is bound to **only one kind of ligand.** E.g. $\ce{ [Co(NH3)6]^3+ }$
2. Heterolyptic complexes → Complexes in which the central metal ion or atom is bonded to **more than one kind of ligand**. $\ce{ [Co(NH3)4Cl2]+ }$
3. Homonuclear Complexes - Complex where only ONE type of metal atom is present
4. Heteronuclear Complexes (not in syllabus)

## Werner's Theory
*Werner ne ek ayesi theory di thi jisse aap oxidation number & cordination snumber de sakte ho*
**Postulate 1-**
- each CMA/CMI in  a complex exhibits two types of valencies
1. Primary Valency → 1° Valency → gives Oxidation Number
2. Seconday Valency → 2°Valency → gives Coordination Number

**Postulate 2 -**
- Primary Valency is non-directional (not responsible for predicting the geometry)
- Secondary Valency is directional (responsible for predicting the geometry)

## Isomerism
↪ Isomers are two or more compounds that have the same chemical formula but a different arrangement of atoms
1. Structural Isomerism
	1. Linkage Isomerism
	2. Coordination Isomerism
	3. Ionisation Isomerism
	4. Solvate Isomerism
2.  Stereoisomerism
	1. Geometric Isomerism
	2. Optical Isomerism

### Linkage Isomerism
Linkage isomerism arises in a coordination compound containing *ambidentate ligand*.
**Example:-** complexes containing the thiocyanate ligand, $\ce{ NCS- }$ , which may bind through the nitrogen to give M–NCS or through sulphur to give M–SCN

### Coordination Isomerism
This type of isomerism arises from the interchange of ligands between cationic and anionic entities of different metal ions present in a complex.
**Example:-**
$\ce{ [Co(NH3)6][Cr(CN)6] }$ its isomer is like $\ce{  [Cr(NH3)6][Co(CN)6], }$

### Ionisation Isomerism
This form of isomerism arises when the counter ion in a complex salt is itself a potential ligand and can displace a ligand which can then become the counter ion.
