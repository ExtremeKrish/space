## Huygen's Wave Theory
Huygens' principle is the basis of wave theory of light. It tells how a wavefront propagates through a medium. According to Huygens' principle, **each point on wavefront is a source of secondary waves**, which add up to give a wavefront at any later time.


Huygen thought that Light is a longitudinal wave and it needs a medium to travel just like sound. But he was wrong.

**Wave Front**
Wave front is always perpendicular to the light ray.
- If there is a point source then wave front will be spherical.
- If there is a line source then wave front will be cylindrical. 
- And if the object is at infinity and the light rays are parallel then wavefront will be planer.

This principle is based on the following assumptions :
1. Each point on a wavefront acts as a **fresh source of new disturbance**, called secondary waves or wavelets.
2. The **secondary wavelets spread out in all directions** with the speed of light in the given medium.
3. The **new wavefront at any later time is given by the forward envelope** (tangential surface in the forward direction) of the secondary wavelets at that time.

## Interferance of Light Waves
**Phase difference ($\Delta \phi$):**  
When two waves have the same wavelength, frequency, and amplitude but are not in the same stage of vibration, the angular difference between them is called phase difference.

If phase difference = 0 → waves are perfectly in sync (constructive interference)  
If phase difference = π → waves are opposite (destructive interference)

**Path difference ($\Delta p$) :**  
The difference in the distance travelled by two waves to reach the same point is called path difference.

### Relation between Phase differnce & Path difference
$$
\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta p
$$

### Principle of Superposition of Waves
If there are two waves i.e. with phase difference $\phi$
- $y_{1} = a_{1} \sin (\omega t)$
- $y_{2} = a_{2} \sin (\omega t + \phi)$
Then the resultant wave's amplitude will be
$$
y = A \sin (\omega t + \theta)
$$
Where A is
$$
A = \sqrt{ a_{1}^2 + a_{2}^2 + 2a_{1}a_{2}\cos \phi }
$$
And $$
\tan \theta = \frac{a_{2}\sin \phi}{a_{1}+a_{2}\cos \phi}
$$
**Intensity:** Amount of energy incident per unit area per unit time. $I \propto A^2$
