**Atoms**
## JJ Thomson's Plum Pudding Model
JJ Thomson revealed that:
1. Atoms of different elements contained negatively charged constituents, (*which are called electrons*) that are identical for all atoms.
2. Atoms on a whole are electrically neutral 
3. An atom must also contains some positive charge to neutralize the negative charge of the electrons. 
**The first model of atom**
First Model of atom was proposed by JJ Thomson,
According to this model :
- The positive charge of the atom is uniformly distributed throughout the volume of atom and 
- The negatively charged electrons are embedded in it like seeds in a watermelon
THIS WAS CALLED THE PLUM PUDDING MODEL OF THE ATOM

The impact parameter is the perpendicular distance of the initial velocity vector of Alpha particle from the centre of the nucleus.

### Electron Orbits
The electrostatic force of attraction $F_{e}$ between the revolving electrons and nucleus provides the required centripetal force ($F_{c}$) to keep them in their orbits.

**Relation between orbit radius and electron velocity** *(in hydrogen atom)*

$$
\begin{align*}
F_{e} &= F_{c} \\
\frac{1}{4\pi \epsilon_{0}} \frac{e^2}{r^{\cancel{2}}} &= \frac{mv^2}{\cancel{r}} \\
\Aboxed{r &= \frac{e^2}{4\pi \epsilon_{0} \; mv^2}}
\end{align*}
$$
**Velocity** (of electron in hydrogen atom)
$$
v = \frac{e}{\sqrt{ 4\pi \epsilon_{0}mr }}
$$
**Total energy of the electron in hydrogen atom**
$$
K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2
 = \frac{e^2}{8\pi \epsilon_{0}\;r}$$
And 
$$
U = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi \epsilon_{0}\;r}
$$
$$
\begin{align*}
E &= K + U \\
&=\frac{e^2}{8\pi \epsilon_{0}\;r} -\frac{e^2}{4\pi \epsilon_{0}\;r}  \\
\Aboxed{E &= -\frac{e^2}{8\pi \epsilon_{0}\;r}}
\end{align*}
$$

Note that all we did above was valid for hydrogen atom only.
## Bohr's Model of The Hydrogen Atom
While the planetary system is held by gravitational force, the nucleus-electron system being charged objects, interact by Coulomb’s Law of force. 

**Drawbacks of Rutherford's Theory**
We know that an *object which moves in a circle is being constantly accelerated* – the acceleration being centripetal in nature. 

According to classical electromagnetic theory, *an accelerating charged particle emits radiation in the form of electromagnetic waves*. The *energy of an accelerating electron should* therefore, *continuously decrease*. 

*The electron would spiral inward and eventually fall into the nucleus*. **Thus, such an atom can not be stable.** 

Further, according to the classical electromagnetic theory, the frequency of the electromagnetic waves emitted by the revolving electrons is equal to the frequency of revolution. As the electrons spiral inwards, their angular velocities and hence their frequencies would change continuously, and so will the frequency of the light emitted. Thus, they would emit a continuous spectrum, in contradiction to the line spectrum actually observed.

### Bohr's Postulates
**First**
An electron in an atom could revolve when certain stable orbits without the emission of radiant energy.
Or, each atom has certain definite stable state in which it can exist.
*Atoms me pakka kuch ayese orbits hote honge jinme electron bina radiant energy emit kiye revolve karega.*

**Second**
Electron revolve around the nucleus only in those orbits for which the angular momentum is some integral multiple of $h/2\pi$.
*Matlab electron nuclear ke around unhi orbits me revolve karega jisme uska angular momentum $h/2\pi$ ka intergral multiple ho.*
$$
L = \frac{nh}{2\pi}
$$
**Third**
An electron might make a transition from one of its specified non radiating orbits to another of lower energy, & when it does so, a photon is emitted having energy equal to the energy difference between the initial and final states.
*Ek electron apne specified non-radiating orbits me se kisi ek se dusre lower energy wale orbit me transition kar sakta hai.
Aur jab wo aisa karta hai, tab ek photon emit hota hai jiska energy, initial aur final states ke beech ke energy difference ke equal hota hai.*

---
We know that Angular Momentum L is mvr
$$
\boxed{L_{n} = mv_{n}r_{n} = \frac{nh}{2\pi}}
$$
where n is an integer, $r_{n}$, is the radius of $n^{th}$ possible orbit and $v_{n}$ is the speed of moving electron in the $n^{th}$ orbit.

Lets find the Velocity & Radius of electron in $n^{th}$ orbit.
So we knew that (for hydrogen atom's innermost & only electron)
$$
r = \frac{e^2}{4\pi \epsilon_{0} mv^2}
$$
Putting that in formula above, we get
$$
\boxed{v_n = \frac{e^2}{2 \epsilon_{0}\; nh}}
$$
& If we again put this $v_{n}$ in the above formula of angular momentum , we get
$$
\boxed{r_{n} = \frac{\epsilon_{\circ} n^2 h^2}{\pi me^2}}
$$
So the size of Innermost Orbit would be
$$
r_{1} = \frac{\epsilon_0 h^2}{\pi m e^2} = a_\circ
$$
**This is also called Bohr's Radius** $a_{0} = 5.3 \times 10^{-11} m$
> And radii of these orbits increases as $n^2$

We can also find the total energy, as we know $E = - e^2/8\pi \epsilon_{0}r$ , putting the value of r we get
$$
E = -\frac{e^2}{8\cancel{\pi} \epsilon_{0}} \frac{\cancel{\pi}me^2}{\epsilon_{0} (nh)^2} = -\frac{me^4}{8\epsilon_{0}^2\; (nh)^2}
$$
And substituting the value of all constants, we get
$$
E_{n} = - \frac{2.18 \times 10^{-18}}{n^2}J
$$
But we also know that $1eV = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \; J$, so we can write it as
$$
E = - \frac{13.6}{n^2} eV
$$

## Line Spectrum of Hydrogen Atom
According to the third postulate of Bohr's model, when an atom makes a transition from the higher energy state with quantum number $n_{i}$ to the lower energy state with quantum number $n_{f}$, ($n_{f} < n_{i}$), the difference of energy is carried away by a photon of frequency v such that
$$
\begin{align*}
h\nu &= E_{n_{i}}-E_{n_{f}} \\
h\nu &= \left( -\frac{me^4}{8\epsilon_{0}^2 h^2 n_{i}^2}\right) - \left( -\frac{me^4}{8\epsilon_{0}^2 h^2 n_{f}^2}\right) \\
\nu_{if}  &= -\frac{me^4}{8\epsilon_{0}^2 h^3} \left( \frac{1}{n_{f}^2} - \frac{1}{n_{i}^2}\right) \\

\end{align*}
$$
