**Chapter 6 — Electromagnetic Induction**
The phenomenon in which *electric current* is generated by varying *magnetic field* is called electromagnetic induction.

## Magnetic Flux
Its similar to Electrix flux but ts measures *the no. of Magnetic Field Lines (MFL) passing through a given area.*
$$
\phi_{B} = \vec{B}\cdot\vec{A} = |B| |A| \cos \theta
$$

↪ Whenever my brother, there is change in Magnetic Flux $\phi_{B}$, EMF or current is induced in the coil 🙌
$$
\mathcal{E} \propto \frac{d\phi_{B}}{dt} \implies \mathcal{E} = \frac{-d\phi_{B}}{dt}

$$
i = \frac{1}{R} \frac{d\phi}{dt}

## Inductance
Inductance is a property of a circuit (or coil) that resists change in current.
Or “How strongly a system can induce emf in itself (or another coil) when current changes”
### Self Induction
*So jab tum ek wire ka coil banate ho to vo ek solenoid ban jata hai aur energy ko store kar sakta hai using magnetic field, like when u change current in that coil, it opposes the current to always make a gradient in current change*

*Current starts flowing in coil, Coil creates magnetic field & That field creates magnetic flux okey?
Now if current tries to change or we change…Then Flux changes &Coil generates EMF now, to oppose that current change*

**Coefficient of Self Induction (L)**
$$
\begin{align*}
\phi &\propto I \\
\phi &= LI \\
L &= \frac{\phi}{I}
\end{align*}
$$
L - Coefficient of Self Induction
$\phi$ - Magnetic Flux
#### Induced emf in an Inductor 
EMF = L × rate of change of current
$$
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{E}_{ind} &= \frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta t} \\
&= \frac{\Delta(Li)}{\Delta t} \\ \\

\Aboxed{\mathcal{E}_{ind} &= L\frac{\Delta i}{\Delta t} }\\
i_{ind} &= \frac{L}{R} \frac{\Delta i}{\Delta t} \\
q_{ind} &= \frac{L}{R} \Delta i


\end{align*}
$$

#### Self Inductance of Solenoid

$$

### Enegy Stored in an Inductor
We know that, $P = \mathcal{E} i$ 
$$
\begin{align*}
P &= \mathcal{E} i \\
\frac{dw}{dt} &= \mathcal{E}i \\
\frac{dw}{dt} &= L \frac{di}{dt} i \\
dw&= L i\, di \\
\int dW &= \int L\,i\,di \\
\int_{0}^{W} dW &= L\int_{0}^{i} i \, di   \\
\Delta U &= L\left[ \frac{i^2}{2} \right]^{i}_{0}  \\
\Aboxed{U &= \frac{1}{2}L\,i^2}
\end{align*}
$$
### Energy Density in an Inductor (u)
$$
\begin{align*}
u &= \frac{U}{V} = \frac{U}{Al}  \\
&= \frac{\frac{1}{2} Li^2}{Al} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}\frac{ (\mu_{0}n^2Al)i^2}{Al} \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \mu_{0}n^2 i^2 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{(\mu_{0}ni)^2}{\mu_{0}} \\
u_{b} &= \frac{B^2}{2 \mu_{0}}
\end{align*}
$$
Here we wrote $\mu_{0}nI$ as B because that's what we learnt in [[Chapter - 4#Application Solenoid|4th Chapter, Ampere's Circuital Law for Solenoid]]

### Mutual Induction
The property of coil by which it opposes the change in flux due to change in the current in other coil.

![[image_search_1776328784839.png|750x449]]

**Coefficient of Mutual Induction (M)**
$$
\begin{align*}
\phi_{2} &\propto i_{1} \\
\phi_{2} &= M_{2,1} \;i_{1} \text{ for single turn} \\
N_{2}\phi_{2} &= M_{2,1} \;i_{1} \text{ for solenoid} \\
M_{2,1} &= \frac{N_{2}\phi_{2}}{i_{1}}
\end{align*}
$$
Also
$$
\phi_{2} = \frac{M_{2}}{N_2}i_{1}
$$
#### Induced EMF by Mutual Induction
$$
\begin{align*}

\mathcal{E} &= \frac{d\phi}{dt} \\
&= \frac{\frac{M_{2}}{N_2}di_{1}}{dt} \\
&= N \times M_{2,1}\; \frac{di_{1}}{dt}
\end{align*}
$$
#### Mutual Induction of Two Co-axial Solenoids
$$
M = \mu_{0}n_{1}n_{2}\pi r^2 l
$$
$n_{1}$ → Number of Turns per unit length
$n_{2}$ → Number of Turns per unit length

## AC Generator
So it generates AC Generator
$$
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{E} &= \frac{d\phi}{dt} \\
&= \frac{d(BA\cos \theta)}{dt} \because \phi = B \cdot A \\
&= \frac{-d (BA\cos(\omega t))}{} \because \omega = \frac{\theta}{t} \\
&= -BA \frac{d(\cos \omega t)}{dt} \\
&= BA(\sin \omega t) \omega  \\
&= BA \omega \; (\sin \omega t) 
\end{align*}
$$
This was for single turn, for N turns
$$
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{E} &= NBA \omega \; (\sin \omega t)  \\
\mathcal{E} &= \mathcal{E}_{0}\; \sin \omega t 
\end{align*}
$$
Where $\mathcal{E}_0 = NBA \omega$ = Max EMF

And we also know that $\mathcal{E} = IR$ so
$$
I = I_{0} \sin \omega t
$$
Where 
$$
I_{0} = \frac{NBA \omega}{R}
$$
