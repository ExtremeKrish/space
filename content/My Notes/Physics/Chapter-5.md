> 600 BCE Greece, there was a city called Magnesia where the people spoke Greek. Near the city a shepherd boy watched his sheep. The story is that he used a stick with an iron tip to climb over stony places. One day, he touched the top to a stone and it happened to stick a little. Could the stone have something sticky on it? A wise man named Thales lived in that area. Today he would be called a scientist. He heard about this stone from Magnesia and had such a stone brought to him. ## Bar Magnet
$\vec{M}$ = Magnetic Dipole Moment
m -> Strength of Pole
$$
M = m\,2l
$$
Where $2l$ is Sepration b/w Poles.
**Units:** M : $Am^2$ & m : $Am$

If there's a U shaped magnet with radius R, dont take circumference, cuz that's distance, take displacement! So it would be:
$$
M = m\,2R
$$
### Cutting of Magnet
1. Cutting along the length of magnet
$$
M' = \frac{M}{2}
$$
1. Cutting perpendicular to the lenght
$$
M' = \frac{M}{2}
$$
### Force b/w two Magnetic Poles
$$
F = \frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi} \frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{r^2}
$$
*Just like Coulomb's Law*
### Magnetic Field due to a pole
$$
B = \frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi}\frac{m}{r^2}
$$

### Force on pole in external magnetic field
$$
F = mB
$$
### Magnetic Field due to a Dipole
#### On Axial Point
$$
B_{net} = \frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi}\frac{2\vec{M}}{r_{3}}
$$
#### On Equitorial Point
$$
B_{net} = \frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi}\frac{\vec{M}}{r_{3}}
$$

### Properties of Magnetic Field Lines
1. Imaginary lines
2. MFL always forms closed loops
3. Tangent represents direction of Magnetic Field $\vec{B}$
4. No Magnetic Field Line can intersect with each other
5. The larger the number of field lines crossing per unit area , the stronger is the magnitude of magnatic field $\vec{B}$
### Current Carrying coil as a Bar Magnet
Aight so Imagine a Coil or a Solenoid, 
Let the number of turns are N, so magnetic field
$$
\begin{align*}
B &= \frac{\mu_{0}}{2} \frac{NIR^2}{(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}} \\
&= \frac{\mu_{0} NIR^2}{2x^3} \; \because [x\gg R] \\
&= \frac{\mu_{0} NIR^2}{2x^3} \times \frac{2\pi}{2\pi} \\
&= \frac{\mu_{0} 2NI\; \pi R^2}{4 \pi x^3} \\
&= \frac{\mu_{0} 2NIA}{4 \pi x^3} \\
&= \frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi} \frac{2m}{x^3}
\end{align*}
$$

## Magnetisation & Magnetic Intensity
### Magnetic Intensity (H)
$$
B_{0} = \mu_{0} ni = \mu_{0}H \\
$$
$$
H = ni = \frac{N}{l}i = \frac{\text{Ampere}}{\text{meter}} = \frac{B_{0}}{\mu_{0}}
$$
### Intensity of Magnetization (I)
$$
I = \frac{\text{Magneti Mom.}}{\text{Volume}} = \frac{ml}{A\times l} = \frac{m}{A}
$$

### Magnetic Permeability ($\mu$)
$$
\mu = \mu_{r}\,\mu_{\circ}
$$
### Magnetic Susceptibility ($\chi$)
$$
\chi = \frac{I}{H}
$$
